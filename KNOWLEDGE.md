# Minecraft basics (Bedrock survival, Easy)

## The day
- A full day is 20 minutes: about 10 of day, 1.5 of sunset, 7 of night, 1.5 of dawn.
- Monsters spawn in the dark: at night outside, and in caves at any time. Zombies and skeletons burn in
  sunlight; spiders and creepers don't.
- Sleeping in a bed skips the night once everyone is in bed, and sets your respawn point. A bed takes 3 wool
  of one colour (from sheep) and 3 planks. A bed is the best present at sunset on day 1.

## Getting started (the usual first hour)
1. Punch trees for logs (5 to 10). Logs make planks (1 log gives 4), planks make sticks (2 give 4) and a
   crafting table (4 planks).
2. Wooden pickaxe, then mine stone straight away and swap to stone tools. Wood tools are only a step.
3. Coal (black-spotted ore, often showing on hillsides and in caves) plus sticks makes torches (4 at a time).
   Torches stop monsters spawning nearby. Light up where people live and where you dig.
4. Food: kill cows, pigs, chickens or sheep; cook the meat in a furnace (8 cobblestone). Cooked meat fills
   you up far more than raw. Bread takes 3 wheat. Apples sometimes drop from oak leaves.
5. A shelter before the first night: even a small dirt or cobblestone hut with a door and a torch is fine.
   Or dig into a hillside and block the entrance.
6. Iron (beige-spotted ore) needs a stone pickaxe or better. Smelt the raw iron in a furnace for iron
   ingots: iron tools, armour, buckets and shields.

## Danger
- **Creeper:** green, silent, hisses, then explodes and breaks blocks. Don't fight one next to a friend or
  a house. Back away, or hit it and step back.
- **Zombie:** slow, groans, comes in groups at night. **Skeleton:** shoots arrows from far; get close or
  take cover. **Spider:** climbs walls; only angry in the dark. **Enderman:** tall and black; never look it
  in the eyes. It's harmless unless you do.
- **Lava** (orange, glowing) is deadly, and is common deep underground. **Falling** more than 3 blocks
  hurts. **Drowning:** come up for air.
- Never dig straight down: you might fall into lava or a cave. Dig in stairs.
- Low health: eat, keep away from monsters, and let health come back.

## Useful to know
- A player dies at 0 health and drops everything where they died. Go back quickly (5 minutes) to collect it.
- Chests keep things safe. Furnaces need fuel: coal is best, and planks or logs work.
- Villages have food, beds and villagers to trade with emeralds. Iron golems protect them.
- Ores: coal (common, high up), copper (orange-green), iron (beige), gold and redstone (deeper),
  diamonds (light blue, very deep, need an iron pickaxe).
- Kids love: animals (name them, feed them wheat to breed cows and sheep, carrots for pigs, seeds for
  chickens), building a house together, finding a village, treasure in chests, a cosy torch-lit base.

## Being a good teammate for a 7-year-old
- Let them lead. Make suggestions as questions ("Want to build a house before dark?").
- Share the good stuff: give them the better sword, torches and cooked food. Never give rotten flesh,
  spider eyes or poisonous potatoes as food: they make you sick.
- When someone asks for something, answer straight away, even if it's "I need to make that first".
- Celebrate their wins. If they die, be kind and help them get their things back.
- Keep it fun and calm at night: "Stay by me, I'll guard you!"

## Building (how builders work; there are no ready-made houses, you place every block)
- Let your friend choose the spot and the style, and build together. Pick flat ground and write the plan in
  your note: the corners and floor level (e.g. "house x 10..16, z 4..9, floor y 63; walls done to y 65").
- Count before you start. A 7 by 6 house with walls 3 high needs about 42 floor blocks, 66 wall blocks and
  42 roof blocks: about 150. One log makes 4 planks. Gather and craft enough first, or build in parts.
- Build from the bottom up: floor, then the walls one layer at a time (every block has to touch another),
  then the roof. Leave a gap 1 wide and 2 high for the door, and gaps at head height for windows.
- Modern style: straight lines, a flat roof (one layer on top of the walls, can stick out by 1), big windows
  (glass: smelt sand in a furnace), light walls (birch planks, smooth stone, white concrete later) with dark
  trim (dark oak planks, stone), lots of light inside (torches, lanterns).
- After each part, compare "built" with your plan; break wrong blocks with dig and put the right ones.
- When something works or fails, write the lesson in your journal so the next house is better.
